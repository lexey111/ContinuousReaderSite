Your data, your devices

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 2026

The short version

ContinuousReader, PlainReader and JustReader do not collect, analyze, or sell any personal data. No analytics, no telemetry, no tracking, no advertising. Your data stays with you — on your own devices, in your own iCloud account if you turn on sync, and briefly in transit between your devices when sync hands a fresh reading position or a changed bookmark from one to another. Nothing else, nowhere else.

Data stored on your device

All app data is stored locally on your device:

  • Books — converted to HTML and stored in your app’s Documents directory. You can browse these files in Finder.
  • Reading positions, bookmarks, notes — stored locally inside the app sandbox: a SwiftData database in ContinuousReader, plain files and preferences in PlainReader and JustReader.
  • Reader settings — font, theme, layout preferences, stored in UserDefaults on each device.
  • Translation history — if translation statistics tracking is enabled, looked-up words are stored locally. You can disable tracking or clear the history at any time.

Sync (optional, ContinuousReader only)

When you turn on sync, your reading data lives in only three places — and travels only between them:

  • Your own devices, locally — the same content stored on each device the app is installed on. This is also exactly what you have when sync is off.
  • Your iCloud account, under your Apple ID. Apple hosts it; the developer has no access. Specifically: book files (converted HTML, cover images, per-book metadata and bookmarks), the library’s structure (folders, OPDS catalogs, tags) and translation records, if you opted into tracking, go to iCloud Drive; a copy of the books’ metadata with your notes, the list of your devices and a backup of your latest reading position go to iCloud Key-Value Store.
  • In flight, between your devices — when you turn a page, the new position is relayed to your other devices through a realtime channel, encrypted in transit. A position message contains a hashed book identifier, a paragraph anchor inside the book, and a progress percentage. When you add, change or remove a bookmark, it travels the same way, so that it shows on your other device in a second rather than in minutes: the name of the book’s folder (its author and title), the place, the colour, the marked passage and your note to it. Messages pass through and are not kept on the channel beyond the short time a reconnection needs. The relay is operated by Ably; tokens that let your devices connect to it are minted by a small Cloudflare Worker. Your account is known to both only as a randomly generated identifier we keep in your iCloud Keychain.

You can disable sync at any time; your library continues to work locally on each device.

Network access

JustReader has essentially no network footprint — only URL import touches the network. PlainReader adds OPDS browsing, and nothing else. ContinuousReader uses the network for sync and OPDS browsing when those are enabled. Specifically:

  • Sync is enabled (ContinuousReader only) — book files with their bookmarks go through Apple iCloud Drive, metadata through Apple iCloud Key-Value Store, and live updates of your position and bookmarks through a realtime channel between your devices, encrypted in transit (see Sync, above, for what each path carries)
  • OPDS catalog browsing (ContinuousReader and PlainReader) — you connect to third-party book catalogs you configure yourself
  • URL import (all three apps) — when you drag a URL into the import dialog to download a book file

During the act of reading, the only traffic is the sync of your position and bookmarks covered above. A position carries only a hashed book ID, a paragraph anchor, and a progress percentage; a bookmark carries the passage you marked and your note as well. Translation is on-device through Apple’s Translation framework — for translation, nothing ever leaves your device.

Third-party services

The apps do not integrate any third-party analytics, crash reporting, advertising, or tracking services. The external dependencies are Apple’s own frameworks (iCloud, Translation), and — only when you turn on sync — Ably (a realtime relay for your reading position and bookmarks, encrypted in transit) and a Cloudflare Worker (which mints short-lived tokens that let your devices connect to that relay). Both know your account only as a random identifier we keep in your iCloud Keychain — never your name, email, or anything tied to your real identity. A bookmark’s message does carry the book’s folder name and the passage you marked; neither service keeps the messages flowing through.

This website and WebReader

This website and WebReader, the reader in the browser at read.continuousreader.app, count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics: how many there were, from which countries and browsers, to which pages. It sets no cookies and keeps nothing that tells who you are. The books you open in WebReader are read in your browser and never uploaded: they reach neither us nor Cloudflare.

NanoReader

NanoReader, the program for Windows, Linux and Mac, counts nothing and sends nothing. It goes to the network only for a book you give it a link to. Your books and everything you marked in them stay on your computer.

Children’s privacy

The apps do not collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted here with a new "Last updated" date. The apps themselves do not collect data, so changes would only reflect new features (like optional cloud services).
