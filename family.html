Three apps, one reading engine

A nightstand, a shelf, a library.

One reader, three ways to keep your books.

What you choose here is not the reading — that part is the same in all three — but what stands around it. So the question is not which app reads better. It is how many books you keep, and what you do with them.

Reading one book at a time, downloading the next when you have finished the last? JustReader. Keeping a month ahead on a shelf and finding the next one in an OPDS catalogue? PlainReader. A real collection — hundreds or thousands of volumes, read across several devices? ContinuousReader. The reading core is the same in all of them; what changes is how the books are kept.

JustReader · free

The nightstand.

One book at a time. Open a file, read it, close it. There is no library, no shelf and nothing to file — the book you are reading is the whole app.

Pick it if
  • One book at a timeNo shelf, no library — and nothing to file.
  • Someone sent you an EPUBIt opens straight away and reads properly.
  • Trying the engineIt is all here. If you later want more room, there is a level up — and only if you decide it is worth it.
  • You come back to booksA week later, a month later — the book opens where you closed it.

Free, with no trial period and the reader complete: every reading feature here is exactly the one the bigger apps have.

The shelf.

A shelf with dividers: up to 999 books in named, coloured, foldable categories. Not a library to maintain — and every book on it keeps its own page open.

Pick it if
  • Several books at onceA novel, a manual and the one you fell asleep in — each in its own place.
  • Just enough orderDividers with names and colours, a filter over the whole shelf — no genres, no sorting: the shelf can still be taken in at a glance.
  • The whole readerEvery theme, every mode, translation and Read Aloud — nothing withheld.
  • Books from cataloguesOPDS is built in: found and downloaded with one button. An author worth following? A series still running? Leave a shortcut in the catalogue and come back to it.

A shelf answers a question a nightstand never raises: what am I reading tomorrow, and what am I coming back to after that?

The library.

Everything is here, and somewhere to put it: folders, colours, genres and series, search across every book at once, and watchers that keep an eye on OPDS catalogues for new arrivals. And a reading position that follows you from the Mac to the iPhone mid-paragraph.

Pick it if
  • Hundreds of booksFolders, colours, genres, series — and a global search through the full text of every book, its annotations and your notes. The one you want takes a second.
  • More than one deviceThe same book and the same paragraph on Mac, iPad and iPhone, both ways.
  • An old collection of a thousand booksMass import takes the whole folder in one go: every candidate is shown before a byte is written, and duplicates come marked in advance.
  • You read seriouslyBuild the reader you want: notes on the left, bookmarks on the right, navigation across the top. Panels that dock, windows that float, a key for everything.

The one to use when reading is not an occasional pastime but a real part of your life.

In all three

The same reading.

The part you actually spend your evening in is literally the same in all three — not three similar readers but one. Whichever you start with, and whenever you change the shell around it, there is nothing to get used to again.

  • The pageScroll, page or two-page spread, real typography, hyphenation and justification, and margins you can set
  • The lookForty-one themes, day and night, including two that dress the whole page — Typewriter, and Terminal in its Norton frame.
  • The wordsTranslation of a selected fragment, Read Aloud with the line lit as it goes, and search inside the book
  • What the book keepsBookmarks with colours, notes and the place you stopped — held for the book, not for the copy on disk.
  • Every deviceNative on Mac, iPad and iPhone, bought once for all three — and no subscription anywhere in the family

Changing the shell means moving books, not learning an app. There is no upgrade path between them: each is its own purchase, and each is bought once. Your reading settings do travel, though — all of them, in one .crsettings file.
