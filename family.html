Three apps, one reading engine

A nightstand, a shelf, a library.

One reader, three ways to keep your books.

What you choose here is not the reading — that part is the same in all three — but what stands around it. So the question is not which app reads better. It is how many books you keep, and what you do with them.

Reading one book at a time, downloading the next when you have finished the last? JustReader. Keeping a month ahead on a shelf and finding the next one in an OPDS catalogue? PlainReader. A real collection — hundreds or thousands of volumes, read across several devices? ContinuousReader. The reading core is the same in all of them; what changes is how the books are kept.

JustReader · free

The nightstand.

One book at a time. Open a file, read it, close it. There is no library, no shelf and nothing to file — the book you are reading is the whole app.

Pick it if
  • You read one book at a timeand have no wish to organise anything
  • Someone sent you an EPUBand you want to read it now, properly
  • You want to try the enginebefore paying for room in it
  • You come back to booksclose one and open it again later — your place is still where you left it

Free, with no trial and nothing held back: the reading is the same reading.

The shelf.

Up to twenty-five numbered places, each holding a book. Not a library to maintain — a shelf you take in at a glance, where every book keeps its own page open.

Pick it if
  • You keep a few books goinga novel, a manual, and the one you fell asleep in
  • You do not want a catalogueno folders, no tags, nothing to file
  • You want the whole readerevery theme, every mode, translation and Read Aloud
  • You take books from cataloguesOPDS is built in — bring one straight from a server into a place

A shelf answers the question a library never asks: what am I reading right now?

The library.

Everything, and somewhere to put it: folders, colours, genres and series, search across every book, catalogues with watchers, statistics — and a reading position that follows you from the Mac to the phone mid-paragraph.

Pick it if
  • You keep hundreds of booksand want to find one in a second
  • You read on more than one devicethe same book, the same paragraph, both ways
  • You write while you reada real editor per book, and marks you can find again
  • You want the workspacepanels to dock, float or pin, and a keyboard shortcut for everything

The one to reach for when reading is not an occasional thing.

In all three

The same reading.

The part you actually spend your evening in is one codebase. Whichever you start with, and whenever you move up, you keep the reader you already know.

  • The pageScroll, page or two-page spread, real typography, hyphenation and justification, and margins you can set
  • The lightEvery theme, day and night, including the two with a frame of their own — Typewriter and Norton
  • The wordsTranslation of a selected fragment, Read Aloud with the line lit as it goes, and search inside the book
  • What you leave behindBookmarks with colours, notes and the place you stopped — kept for the book, not for the copy on disk
  • Every deviceNative on Mac, iPad and iPhone, bought once for all three — and no subscription anywhere in the family

Moving up means moving books, not learning an app. There is no upgrade path between them: each is its own purchase, and each is bought once.
