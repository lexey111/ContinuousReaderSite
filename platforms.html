Mac · iPad · iPhone

Three platforms, three experiences.

One app across Mac, iPad, and iPhone — adapted to each device’s way of working, not mediocre on all three.

Cross-platform apps usually land in one of two places. Either they look the same everywhere — which means they look wrong somewhere. Or they’re three separate apps held together by the same name and a cloud icon.

ContinuousReader is neither. The reading engine, the library, the typography, the themes, translation, Read Aloud — one shared codebase, and it behaves the same on every device. What changes is how you reach it: keyboard and trackpad on the Mac, taps and swipes on the iPad, a single thumb on the iPhone — and two readers to choose from on all three, Comfort and Professional.

macOS

The full workspace.

The Mac version takes everything the platform offers: a key for anything you do often, panels that dock to any edge, float as windows of their own or pin, the Book Card and the browsers — authors, series, notes, journal, statistics — in windows of their own, drag-and-drop just about everywhere, and an island design borrowed from Apple’s System Settings.

What Mac gets uniquely
  • Table viewSortable columns, a layout remembered per tab, folders among the rows, an action row above the table
  • Up to four columnsA wide window reads like a newspaper: two, three or four columns in Pages mode, the count following the font size
  • Keyboard shortcutsFor everything: ⌘B to bookmark, ⌘F to search, ⌘G for the progress drawer, F2–F4 for panels, Space for quick preview
  • Panels and docksEvery panel docks to any edge, floats or pins; layouts move them all at once; a double Esc opens the panel selector, a double F9 the settings navigator
  • Custom library locationPoint the app at any folder on your Mac or an external drive
  • Portable libraryThe whole library is one self-contained folder — copy it anywhere for a backup, or import a Calibre library as it is
  • Browsers in windowsAuthors, series, notes & highlights, the reading journal and statistics open as library tabs or in windows of their own, beside the book

The version to use when you have a keyboard, a chair, and time.

iPadOS

The reading surface.

The iPad sits between the Mac and the iPhone, and gets the best of both: with a keyboard attached it is the Mac’s reader — panels, docks, the same keys; put the keyboard away and it reads like the iPhone, full screen with active corners.

What iPad does uniquely well
  • Native swipe actionsOn books, for quick actions — pin, colour, delete
  • Panels that dockBookmarks, chapters, notes, search, translation, pictures — docked to any edge, floating or pinned; the same data as on the Mac
  • Split view readyRead alongside any other iPad app
  • Columns in landscapeTurn the iPad on its side and the page opens as a spread of two or three columns — the wide screen put to work, not a stretched-out phone layout
  • The Mac’s keysWith a keyboard attached, a double Esc, H for Quiet mode, the tab selector and the panel selector work as on the Mac
  • The library’s tabsAuthors, Journal, Statistics and Notes & Highlights on the tab row; OPDS at full page size
  • Full screen when you want itA tap in the centre hides the chrome; the bar with the clock, progress and battery is yours to assemble
UI: English

Same island design as the Mac, same fonts, same themes. The library looks like it belongs to the same app — because it does.

iOS

The pocket reader.

The iPhone version is streamlined, not stripped. Everything that earns its place on a small screen is here; the table view and the floating panels sit it out, because cramming them in would only make the app worse. In their place: full-screen reading with active corners, tiles instead of panels, native sheets — and the library’s tabs, browsers included, laid out for a phone.

What iPhone does differently
  • One-hand friendlyControls within thumb’s reach. Two page-turn layouts — top/bottom for one thumb or left/right for two — and a “Show tap zones” overlay that draws them onto the page so you never have to guess
  • Native sheetsFolders, settings, filters, the browsers — sheets of the right height instead of separate windows
  • Always full screenThe page takes the whole screen. Professional has two active corners — settings at the bottom-left, navigation tiles at the bottom-right; Comfort has one corner and one menu
  • A bottom bar you assembleSession time, clock, battery and your position — a reading for every slot, in the style you pick, with a live preview
  • The library on a phoneTabs as a history, the Authors, Journal and Notes browsers re-laid for the screen, rows that tighten as the list grows, cards two to four across
  • Gesture navigationEdge swipes, swipe-to-dismiss sheets. iOS the way iOS should work
  • Sink and CardsThe phone’s page turns — one card the screen wide — beside Slide

Common ground

Same reader, every screen.

Reading engine, position sync, 42 themes, 9 fonts, translation, Read Aloud, the journal and the marks — same code, same behaviour on all three platforms.

  • Two readersComfort and Professional on every device, each with its own settings and a door to the other; Scroll or Pages, with columns where the screen has room.
  • UI that adapts to the screenWider columns where there is room, compact rows where there is not — the same reader, the right amount of it for the device.
  • The same controls, everywhereFonts, size, line height, margins, paragraph spacing, indentation, hyphenation, alignment, images, paper and page turns — every typography and layout control is on the Mac, the iPad and the iPhone.
